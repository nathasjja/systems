Axiom Task Manager: from a weekly meeting log to an app the site team uses

03 / Case study
Client
PT Axiom Construction Co, Bali
Role
Director; designed and delivered it
Team
English and Russian speakers
Timeline
Oct – Nov 2026
● The gap

Action items buried in a meeting log

Axiom is a team of experienced engineers doing interiors and renovations across four live projects: Villas Seseh, 8SENCE, Angel Cakes Uluwatu and Anantara Dragon. There was no task system. Actions lived inside the weekly protocol, one tab per project, in long status-log cells with no deadlines anyone could track.

The team works in English and Russian. And the protocol couldn't be touched: people were still using it every week.

● The thread

One record, linked to every stage

A task manager in Google Sheets with Apps Script, then a custom mobile web app on top. The protocol stays the meeting and finance record. The new system holds every action item, linked to its project stage.

● The gap

A working sheet that made people retype everything

The studio already ran on a Google Sheet: a bookings log, a client CRM and an income dashboard. Ludwig: a language app where the teacher's lessons become the student's homework

05 / Case study
Client
Private Indonesian tutoring
Role
Founder; designed and built it
Stack
Vite · Supabase · Netlify · GitHub
Timeline
November 2026
● The gap

Lessons and practice lived in different places

A student learning Indonesian from Russian had good lessons and generic apps, and nothing connecting the two. Words taught on Tuesday never showed up in Wednesday's practice, and the teacher couldn't see what had stuck.

● The thread

One app, two sides of the same lesson

Students sign up, wait for approval, then practise word decks with spaced repetition and read short stories with questions. They switch between English and Russian explanations.

The teacher approves students, logs the words taught in each lesson, keeps private notes, and writes decks and stories in the app instead of code. A word logged after class appears in that student's Dari guru ("from the teacher") deck straight away.

It started as a lean practice app for one learner and grew into a platform for several students in the same week, without changing hosts or adding a server.

Petty Cash Backfill: ten weeks of WhatsApp receipts, reconciled

06 / Case study
Client
ALL X, Bali operations
Role
Operations Manager; designed and ran it
Method
AI desktop automation, human checkpoints
Status
● RECONCILED · ENTRY IN PROGRESS
● The gap
Rp1.043.000

in petty cash spend that existed only as forwarded receipts in a WhatsApp group. 39 ride and delivery receipts from 31 August to 9 October 2026 had never reached the ledger.

Untracked spend by periodRp · receipts
Tap a period for detail
TotalRp1.043.00039
● The thread

Three systems the team already used, made into one record

Receipts arrived in a WhatsApp group. The ledger was a Google Sheet with a tab per month and a tab per person. Files belonged in month-dated Drive folders. Nothing connected them, so receipts sat in the chat until someone found time to scroll back.

The automation drove the WhatsApp desktop app directly, exported each receipt, and prepared a dated ledger row for it, with the file linked into the Receipt column as a chip.

It learned the house rules instead of inventing new ones

Categories, payment types, the DD/MM/YYYY date format and the tab layout were all read from rows the team had already written. The hardest rule was never written down: receipts print street addresses, while the team says nicknames. Matching the same address across different messages rebuilt that map, so later receipts filed themselves by address alone.

The pipeline

Caught by re-checking every cluster boundary against WhatsApp's document index, and the original PDF where a caption disagreed.

Human checkpoints

Two calls went back to a person instead of being guessed: a backlog of rides booked under an unfamiliar staff name, and two rides the ride app itself marked Profile: PERSONAL. Those were flagged, not logged.

Time saved is measured, not estimated: it is set against a teammate's average DeskTime activity in Google Sheets for tracking these expenses, uploading receipts and updating the petty cash sheet by hand.
